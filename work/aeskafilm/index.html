Summary

The photos that matter are on guests' phones

At a wedding or graduation, the best candid photos are taken by guests and mostly never reach the couple. Shared albums need an app, an account, or someone chasing links the week after.

AeskaFilm puts a QR card on every table. Guests scan it, give their name, and get a roll of shots in their phone's browser. They don't install anything, they can't retake a shot, and nobody sees the photos until the reveal. The roll develops into a scrapbook-style book that opens the next morning, or live if the client prefers.

Stop 1 · Scan

A card on every table

Each table gets its own QR card, printable from the client's book. Scanning opens the camera page directly. The only thing a guest types is their name, so their shots are credited in the book.

7 left

Stop 2 · Capture

A fixed roll, and no preview

The camera runs in the browser, so there's nothing to install. Each guest gets a set number of shots and can't review or retake them. That keeps people looking at the room instead of their screen, and keeps the book from filling with ten copies of one pose.

Stop 3 · Reveal

The roll develops overnight

Nobody sees anything until the reveal. Then the couple or graduate opens a book with gallery, moments and guest pages, and can print keepsake designs. The client chooses a surprise next morning or a same-day reveal.

What I did

Who uses it

  • Studio admins create a book per event, set package limits (shots per guest, guest cap, table codes, total photo cap), configure the camera, gallery, reveal and projector wall, moderate and export.
  • Clients, the couple or graduate, get a password-protected book with gallery, moments and guests, generate their own table QR cards, and flip the reveal switch.
  • Guests only ever see a camera while capture is open, and the public gallery after the reveal.

Built with Next.js on Cloudflare Workers, Neon Postgres, and R2 for photo storage. Locally it runs against Postgres and an S3-compatible store in Docker, and the deploy refuses to run while local env files are present.
